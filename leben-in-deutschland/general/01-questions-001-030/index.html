    General Questions 1-30

    Questions 1 to 30 of the official catalog. Answer first, then open the solution. Every question is bilingual and carries its own vocabulary table.

    Frage 1

    Frage: In Deutschland dürfen Menschen offen etwas gegen die Regierung sagen, weil …
    English: In Germany, people are allowed to openly say something against the government because …

    # Antwort English
    a hier Religionsfreiheit gilt. freedom of religion applies here.
    b die Menschen Steuern zahlen. people pay taxes.
    c die Menschen das Wahlrecht haben. people have the right to vote.
    d hier Meinungsfreiheit gilt. freedom of speech applies here.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    d) hier Meinungsfreiheit gilt.
    freedom of speech applies here.

    Warum / Why: Freedom of speech (Meinungsfreiheit) is one of the most important fundamental rights guaranteed by Article 5 of the German Basic Law (Grundgesetz). It means every person in Germany has the right to freely express their opinions in speech, writing, and images - including criticism of the government. This right is essential to a functioning democracy.

    Meinungsfreiheit freedom of speech
    gelten to apply / to be valid
    Religionsfreiheit freedom of religion
    Steuer the tax
    zahlen to pay
    Wahlrecht the right to vote

    Frage 2

    Frage: In Deutschland können Eltern bis zum 14. Lebensjahr ihres Kindes entscheiden, ob es in der Schule am …
    English: In Germany, parents can decide until their child turns 14 whether the child takes part in ... at school.

    # Antwort English
    a Geschichtsunterricht teilnimmt. history classes.
    b Religionsunterricht teilnimmt. religious education classes.
    c Politikunterricht teilnimmt. politics classes.
    d Sprachunterricht teilnimmt. language classes.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    b) Religionsunterricht teilnimmt.
    religious education classes.

    Warum / Why: Under German law, parents have the right to decide whether their child participates in religious education at school until the child turns 14. After age 14, the child can decide for themselves whether to attend religious classes (this is called "Religionsmündigkeit" - religious maturity). All other school subjects like history, politics, and languages are mandatory.

    Frage 3

    Frage: Deutschland ist ein Rechtsstaat. Was ist damit gemeint?
    English: Germany is a constitutional state (Rechtsstaat). What does this mean?

    # Antwort English
    a Alle Einwohnerinnen/Einwohner und der Staat müssen sich an die Gesetze halten. All residents and the state must abide by the laws.
    b Der Staat muss sich nicht an die Gesetze halten. The state does not have to abide by the laws.
    c Nur Deutsche müssen die Gesetze befolgen. Only Germans must obey the laws.
    d Die Gerichte machen die Gesetze. The courts make the laws.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    a) Alle Einwohnerinnen/Einwohner und der Staat müssen sich an die Gesetze halten.
    All residents and the state must abide by the laws.

    Warum / Why: The term "Rechtsstaat" (rule of law / constitutional state) means that the government itself is bound by the law - it cannot act arbitrarily. Everyone, including the state and all its institutions, must follow the law. This principle is a cornerstone of German democracy and is established in the Basic Law.

    Frage 4

    Frage: Welches Recht gehört zu den Grundrechten in Deutschland?
    English: Which right is one of the fundamental rights in Germany?

    # Antwort English
    a Waffenbesitz Right to bear arms
    b Faustrecht Vigilantism
    c Meinungsfreiheit Freedom of expression
    d Selbstjustiz Self-help justice
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    c) Meinungsfreiheit
    Freedom of expression

    Warum / Why: Freedom of expression (Meinungsfreiheit) is a fundamental right protected by Article 5 of the Basic Law. The other options - the right to bear arms, vigilantism (literally "fist law"), and self-help justice - are not fundamental rights in Germany. In fact, vigilantism and self-help justice are illegal. Germany does not have a constitutional right to bear arms like the United States.

    Frage 5

    Frage: Wahlen in Deutschland sind frei. Was bedeutet das?
    English: Elections in Germany are free. What does this mean?

    # Antwort English
    a Man darf Geld annehmen, wenn man dafür eine bestimmte Kandidatin/einen bestimmten Kandidaten wählt. You are allowed to accept money if you vote for a specific candidate.
    b Nur Personen, die noch nie im Gefängnis waren, dürfen wählen. Only people who have never been in prison are allowed to vote.
    c Die Wählerin/der Wähler darf bei der Wahl weder beeinflusst noch zu einer bestimmten Stimmabgabe gezwungen werden und keine Nachteile durch die Wahl haben. Voters must not be influenced or forced to vote in a certain way, nor suffer any disadvantages as a result of their vote.
    d Alle wahlberechtigten Personen müssen wählen. All eligible voters must vote.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    c) Die Wählerin/der Wähler darf bei der Wahl weder beeinflusst noch zu einer bestimmten Stimmabgabe gezwungen werden und keine Nachteile durch die Wahl haben.
    Voters must not be influenced or forced to vote in a certain way, nor suffer any disadvantages as a result of their vote.

    Warum / Why: German elections follow five principles: they are universal, direct, free, equal, and secret (allgemein, unmittelbar, frei, gleich, geheim). "Free" means voters cannot be pressured, bribed, or forced to vote in any particular way. There is no compulsory voting in Germany - voting is a right, not an obligation. Accepting money for votes is illegal.

    Frage 6

    Frage: Wie heißt die deutsche Verfassung?
    English: What is the name of the German constitution?

    # Antwort English
    a Volksgesetz People's Law
    b Bundesgesetz Federal Law
    c Deutsches Gesetz German Law
    d Grundgesetz Basic Law
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    d) Grundgesetz
    Basic Law

    Warum / Why: The German constitution is called the "Grundgesetz" (Basic Law). It was adopted on May 23, 1949 and was originally intended as a temporary constitution for West Germany until reunification. After reunification in 1990, it became the constitution for all of Germany. It was deliberately not called "Verfassung" (constitution) to emphasize its provisional nature, but today it functions fully as Germany's constitution.

    Gesetz the law

    Frage 8

    Frage: Was steht nicht im Grundgesetz von Deutschland?
    English: What is NOT in the German Basic Law?

    # Antwort English
    a Die Würde des Menschen ist unantastbar. Human dignity is inviolable.
    b Alle sollen gleich viel Geld haben. Everyone should have the same amount of money.
    c Jeder Mensch darf seine Meinung sagen. Every person is allowed to express their opinion.
    d Alle sind vor dem Gesetz gleich. All are equal before the law.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    b) Alle sollen gleich viel Geld haben.
    Everyone should have the same amount of money.

    Warum / Why: The Basic Law guarantees human dignity (Article 1), freedom of expression (Article 5), and equality before the law (Article 3). However, it does NOT guarantee that everyone should have the same amount of money. Germany has a social market economy which allows for economic differences while providing a social safety net.

    Frage 9

    Frage: Welches Grundrecht gilt in Deutschland nur für Ausländerinnen/Ausländer? Das Grundrecht auf …
    English: Which fundamental right in Germany applies only to foreigners? The fundamental right to …

    # Antwort English
    a Schutz der Familie protection of the family
    b Menschenwürde human dignity
    c Asyl asylum
    d Meinungsfreiheit freedom of opinion
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    c) Asyl

    Warum / Why: The right to asylum (Article 16a of the Basic Law) is the only fundamental right that applies exclusively to foreigners. It gives politically persecuted people the right to seek protection in Germany. All other listed rights - family protection, human dignity, and freedom of opinion - apply to all people in Germany, regardless of nationality.

    Frage 10

    Frage: Was ist mit dem deutschen Grundgesetz vereinbar?
    English: What is compatible with the German Basic Law?

    # Antwort English
    a die Prügelstrafe corporal punishment
    b die Folter torture
    c die Todesstrafe the death penalty
    d die Geldstrafe a fine
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    d) die Geldstrafe
    a fine

    Warum / Why: Among the listed options, only fines (Geldstrafe) are compatible with the Basic Law. Corporal punishment, torture, and the death penalty are all prohibited. Article 102 explicitly states: "The death penalty is abolished." Article 1 (human dignity) and Article 104 prohibit torture and physical punishment by the state.

    Frage 12

    Frage: Eine Partei im Deutschen Bundestag will die Pressefreiheit abschaffen. Ist das möglich?
    English: A party in the German Bundestag wants to abolish press freedom. Is that possible?

    # Antwort English
    a Ja, wenn mehr als die Hälfte der Abgeordneten im Bundestag dafür sind. Yes, if more than half of the members of the Bundestag are in favor.
    b Ja, aber dazu müssen zwei Drittel der Abgeordneten im Bundestag dafür sein. Yes, but two-thirds of the members of the Bundestag must be in favor.
    c Nein, denn die Pressefreiheit ist ein Grundrecht. Sie kann nicht abgeschafft werden. No, because press freedom is a fundamental right. It cannot be abolished.
    d Nein, denn nur der Bundesrat kann die Pressefreiheit abschaffen. No, because only the Bundesrat can abolish press freedom.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    c) Nein, denn die Pressefreiheit ist ein Grundrecht. Sie kann nicht abgeschafft werden.
    No, because press freedom is a fundamental right. It cannot be abolished.

    Warum / Why: Press freedom is guaranteed by Article 5 of the Basic Law and is protected by the "eternity clause" (Ewigkeitsklausel, Article 79 Paragraph 3). This clause means that certain core principles of the Basic Law - including fundamental rights like press freedom - can never be abolished or changed, not even with a unanimous vote in parliament.

    Frage 14

    Frage: Meinungsfreiheit in Deutschland heißt, dass ich …
    English: Freedom of speech in Germany means that I …

    # Antwort English
    a Passanten auf der Straße beschimpfen darf. am allowed to insult passersby on the street.
    b meine Meinung im Internet äußern kann. can express my opinion on the internet.
    c Nazi-, Hamas- oder Islamischer Staat-Symbole öffentlich tragen darf. am allowed to publicly display Nazi, Hamas, or Islamic State symbols.

However, this right has limits: you cannot insult individuals (that is a criminal offense called "Beleidigung"), and displaying symbols of banned organizations like the Nazis, Hamas, or ISIS is illegal in Germany. Note that this question was updated to include Hamas and Islamic State references in recent catalog versions.

    English: What does the German Basic Law prohibit?

    # Antwort English

However, criticizing the government, discussing religions, or criticizing the state are all protected by freedom of speech. The balance between free speech and personal rights is an important principle in German law.

    Frage 17

    Frage: Die deutschen Gesetze verbieten …
    English: German laws prohibit …

    # Antwort English
    a Meinungsfreiheit der Einwohnerinnen und Einwohner. Freedom of opinion for residents.
    b Petitionen der Bürgerinnen und Bürger. Petitions by citizens.
    c Versammlungsfreiheit der Einwohnerinnen und Einwohner. Freedom of assembly for residents.
    d Ungleichbehandlung der Bürgerinnen und Bürger durch den Staat. Unequal treatment of citizens by the state.
    Lösung / Answer - open after you have chosen

    d) Ungleichbehandlung der Bürgerinnen und Bürger durch den Staat.
    Unequal treatment of citizens by the state.

    Warum / Why: German law prohibits the state from treating citizens unequally (discrimination). This is established in Article 3 of the Basic Law: "All persons are equal before the law." Freedom of opinion, the right to petition, and freedom of assembly are all protected rights - they are NOT prohibited.
