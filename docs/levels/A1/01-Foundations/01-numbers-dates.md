# Numbers, Dates & Spelling - The Easy Way

> Why this matters: **Hören Part 1** is almost always "write down the number/time/word you hear."
> Numbers are the #1 easiest points in the whole exam. Let's make them automatic.

---

## 1. Numbers 1-20 (the foundation - say these in your sleep)

| # | German | # | German |
|---|--------|---|--------|
| 1 | eins | 11 | elf |
| 2 | zwei | 12 | zwölf |
| 3 | drei | 13 | dreizehn |
| 4 | vier | 14 | vierzehn |
| 5 | fünf | 15 | fünfzehn |
| 6 | sechs | 16 | sechzehn |
| 7 | sieben | 17 | siebzehn |
| 8 | acht | 18 | achtzehn |
| 9 | neun | 19 | neunzehn |
| 10 | zehn | 20 | zwanzig |

!!! tip "Trick for 13-19"
    13-19 = number + "zehn" (ten). Easy.

!!! warning "Watch out: 16 and 17"
    NOT sechszehn / siebenzehn. They lose letters: **sechzehn**, **siebzehn**.

## 2. Numbers 20-99 (the "reversed" rule)

**TENS FIRST, ones LAST, connected with "und"**

- 21 = ein**und**zwanzig (one-and-twenty)
- 34 = vier**und**dreißig (four-and-thirty)
- 67 = sieben**und**sechzig

The tens (20-90):

| 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 90 |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| zwanzig | dreißig | vierzig | fünfzig | sechzig | siebzig | achtzig | neunzig |

**Watch out:** 30 = dreißig (one ß, not drei-zig-style). 70 = siebzig (loses "en"). 60 = sechzig.

**Remember the pattern for any number:**
**7** = sieben → **70** = siebzig (loses the "en")
**16** = sechzehn → **60** = sechzig

---

## 3. Numbers 100-1000

- 100 = einhundert
- 200 = zweihundert
- 150 = einhundertfünfzig
- 500 = fünfhundert
- 1000 = eintausend

**Price trick:** Germans are sloppy - "einhundert" often becomes just "hundert", "eintausend" becomes "tausend".

**Just listen for:**

- "hundert" = hundreds
- "tausend" = thousands

## 4. Prices (money - SUPER common in listening + speaking)

"x Euro", "x Euro und x Cent" (or just "x fünfzig" = x.50)

| Spoken | Means |
|--------|-------|
| drei Euro | 3 € |
| zwei Euro fünfzig / zwei fünfzig | 2.50 € |
| fünfundzwanzig Cent | 0.25 € |
| neun Euro neunundneunzig | 9.99 € |

**Tip for listening:** If you hear a price, you almost never need the exact cents - just the euros + big part. Don't panic.

## 5. Days of the Week (Hören loves these)

## 7. Dates & Years (important for form filling + listening)

**A date = "der" + number (ordinal).**

| Spoken (ordinal) | Calendar form | Means |
|------------------|---------------|-------|
| der erste | 1. | the 1st |
| der dritte | 3. | the 3rd |
| der fünfte | 5. | the 5th |
| der siebte | 7. | the 7th |
| der elfte | 11. | the 11th |
| der zwölfte | 12. | the 12th |
| der letzte Tag | 31. | the 31st (letzte) |

**Pattern:** most ordinals = number + "te" (der zweite, der neunte, der elfte).
**Watch out:** 1, 3, 7, 8 change: erste, dritte, siebte, achte.

**Birthday (Geburtsdatum) - you MUST know yours:**

- "Mein Geburtstag ist am einundzwanzigsten September." = My birthday is on 21st September. (ordinal + "ten")

- 1985 = neunzehnhundertfünfundachtzig (nineteen-hundred-eighty-five)
- 2024 = zweitausendvierundzwanzig

**Hören tip:** when they say a date, they always repeat it - listen twice, write once after the repeat.

## 8. Time (die Uhrzeit) - THE COMPLETE GUIDE

> This is one of the trickiest things in German for English speakers.
> German counts time differently from English. Master this and you're ahead of most learners.

### The golden rule: German looks FORWARD, not backward

In English, at 8:30 you say "half PAST eight" (looking back to 8).
In German, at 8:30 you say "halb NEUN" (looking forward to 9).

**halb = half (toward the NEXT hour)**

This is the #1 mistake English speakers make. Burn this into your brain:
- halb eins = 12:30 (half toward ONE)
- halb zwei = 1:30 (half toward TWO)
- halb drei = 2:30 (half toward THREE)
- halb neun = 8:30 (half toward NINE)

### The 6 building blocks of German time

| Building block | German | What it means | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------------|---------|
| Exact hour | Es ist X Uhr. | It's X o'clock | Es ist 8 Uhr. (It's 8:00) |
| Half past | Es ist halb X. | Half toward X | Es ist halb neun. (It's 8:30) |
| Quarter past | Es ist Viertel nach X. | Quarter after X | Es ist Viertel nach acht. (It's 8:15) |
| Quarter to | Es ist Viertel vor X. | Quarter before X | Es ist Viertel vor neun. (It's 8:45) |
| Minutes past | Es ist X nach Y. | X minutes after Y | Es ist fünf nach acht. (It's 8:05) |
| Minutes to | Es ist X vor Y. | X minutes before Y | Es ist zehn vor neun. (It's 8:50) |

### The "halb" system - FULL TABLE (memorize this)

**Rule: halb + NEXT hour = the :30 of the PREVIOUS hour**

| You hear | You write | English | Why? |
|----------|-----------|---------|------|
| halb eins | 12:30 | half past twelve | half toward ONE |
| halb zwei | 1:30 | half past one | half toward TWO |
| halb drei | 2:30 | half past two | half toward THREE |
| halb vier | 3:30 | half past three | half toward FOUR |
| halb fünf | 4:30 | half past four | half toward FIVE |
| halb sechs | 5:30 | half past five | half toward SIX |
| halb sieben | 6:30 | half past six | half toward SEVEN |
| halb acht | 7:30 | half past seven | half toward EIGHT |
| halb neun | 8:30 | half past eight | half toward NINE |
| halb zehn | 9:30 | half past nine | half toward TEN |
| halb elf | 10:30 | half past ten | half toward ELEVEN |
| halb zwölf | 11:30 | half past eleven | half toward TWELVE |

### The "Viertel" system

| You hear | You write | English |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Viertel nach eins | 1:15 | quarter past one |
| Viertel nach zwei | 2:15 | quarter past two |
| Viertel vor drei | 2:45 | quarter to three |
| Viertel vor vier | 3:45 | quarter to four |

### The "nach / vor" system (for other minutes)

| You hear | You write | English |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| fünf nach acht | 8:05 | five past eight |
| zehn nach acht | 8:10 | ten past eight |
| fünf vor neun | 8:50 | five to nine |
| zehn vor neun | 8:50 | ten to nine |
| fünf nach halb neun | 8:35 | five past half past eight |
| zehn nach halb neun | 8:40 | ten past half past eight |

### The 24-hour clock (official times)

Germans use the 24-hour clock for trains, appointments, opening hours.

| Spoken | Written | English |
|--------|---------|---------|
| achtzehn Uhr | 18:00 | 6 PM |
| vierzehn Uhr dreiundvierzig | 14:43 | 2:43 PM |
| null Uhr | 0:00 | midnight |
| zwölf Uhr | 12:00 | noon |

**How to read 14:43:** "vierzehn Uhr dreiundvierzig" (fourteen hours forty-three)
**How to read 8:05:** "acht Uhr fünf" (eight hours five)

### Asking about time

| German | English |
|--------|---------|
| Wie spät ist es? | What time is it? |
| Um wie viel Uhr...? | At what time...? |
| Um wie viel Uhr fährt der Zug? | What time does the train leave? |
| Wann fängt der Film an? | When does the film start? |

### Time prepositions (from prepositions.md)

| German | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| um + Uhrzeit | at (clock time) | um 8 Uhr, um halb neun |
| am + Tag | on (day) | am Montag, am Wochenende |
| im + Monat/Jahr | in (month/year) | im September, im Jahr 2025 |
| von...bis | from...to | von 8 bis 18 Uhr |

### Common time phrases (for speaking + writing)

| German | English |
|--------|---------|
| Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf. | I get up at 7. |
| Ich arbeite von 9 bis 17 Uhr. | I work from 9 to 5. |
| Wir treffen uns um 15 Uhr. | We meet at 3 PM. |
| Der Kurs fängt um 9 Uhr an. | The course starts at 9. |
| Die Apotheke ist von 8 bis 18 Uhr geöffnet. | The pharmacy is open from 8 to 6. |
| Ich komme um 18 Uhr nach Hause. | I come home at 6 PM. |
| Ich gehe um 23 Uhr ins Bett. | I go to bed at 11 PM. |
| Morgen um diese Zeit! | Tomorrow at this time! |

!!! danger "Exam trap: halb"
    If you hear `halb vier` and write 4:30, you are **WRONG**. halb vier = 3:30 (half toward four). The number you hear is ONE AHEAD.

### The exam trap: "halb" in Hören

**In the listening test, they WILL say "halb" something.**
If you hear "halb vier" and write 4:30, you're WRONG.
- halb vier = 3:30 (half toward four)
- The number you hear is ONE AHEAD of what you write.

**Quick trick:** When you hear "halb X", subtract 1 from X for the hour.
- halb acht → acht minus 1 = 7 → 7:30
- halb eins → eins minus 1 = 12 → 12:30 (but since there's no "halb null", this wraps around)

### Opening hours (very common in reading + Hören)

| German | English |
|--------|---------|
| von 8 bis 18 Uhr geöffnet | open from 8 AM to 6 PM |
| montags bis freitags | Monday to Friday |
| sonntags geschlossen | closed on Sundays |
| geöffnet von 9 bis 17 Uhr | open from 9 to 5 PM |
| tagtäglich von 7 bis 22 Uhr | daily from 7 AM to 10 PM |

## 9. Spelling / Buchstabieren (give me my name: HOT TEST AREA)

**Hören almost always spells someone's name. You must recognize the letters.**

Key letters that Germans spell differently than you expect:
- **Z** = "tset" (sounds like "tseht")
- **V** = "fau"
- **W** = "veh" (sounds like English V!)
- **ß** = "Eszett"
- **Ü** = "U-Umlaut", **Ö** = "O-Umlaut", **Ä** = "A-Umlaut"

**Practice one:**
"Ich buchstabiere: H-A-M-E-D." → hmm, but H isn't the issue, it's similar.

**Actually saying the alphabet (listen for challenging ones):**
A=a, B=beh, C=tseh, D=deh, E=eh, F=eff, G=geh, H=hah, I=ee, J=yoht, K=kah, L=ell, M=emm, N=enn, O=oh, P=peh, Q=koo, R=err, S=ess, T=teh, U=oo, V=fau, W=veh, X=iks, Y=uepsilon, Z=tset

**Why this matters:** In Sprechen Part 1 you spell YOUR name and YOUR city. In Hören you hear somebody else's spelled name.

**Your own words to practice OUT LOUD daily:**

- Your first name + last name
- Your street address
- Your city (München!)
- Your email address (sounds like... the base name, no special rules - you say letters only)

## 10. Quick-cheat to survive ANY number question

1. Numbers 1-20 → memorize cold
2. Big numbers → listen for "hundert" / "tausend"
3. Prices → euros first, don't stress cents
4. Dates → "der ...te" + month, or just digits on the form
5. Times → listen for "halb / Viertel / nach / vor"
6. Names → recognize spellings Z, V, W, ß, Ü

!!! success "You are done"
    That's the whole numbers story. Numbers 1-1000, halb/Viertel/nach/vor, dates, spelling - you never need numbers above 1000 at A1. Practice saying them OUT LOUD until automatic.
