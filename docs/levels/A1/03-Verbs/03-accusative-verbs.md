# German Accusative Verbs

## Introduction

German cases usually work like this:

- **Nominative:** the subject, who does the action?
- **Accusative:** often the direct object, whom or what?
- **Dative:** often the receiver or indirect object, to or for whom?

Learn the verb together with a complete sentence chunk. Do not calculate every sentence from scratch.

## Accusative pronouns

| Meaning | Accusative pronoun |
|---|---|
| me | **mich** |
| you, informal singular | **dich** |
| him | **ihn** |
| her | **sie** |
| it | **es** |
| us | **uns** |
| you, informal plural | **euch** |
| them | **sie** |
| you, formal | **Sie** |

## Pronoun comparison

| Person | Nominative | Accusative | Dative |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | ich | **mich** | mir |
| you, informal singular | du | **dich** | dir |
| he | er | **ihn** | ihm |
| she | sie | **sie** | ihr |
| it | es | **es** | ihm |
| we | wir | **uns** | uns |
| you, informal plural | ihr | **euch** | euch |
| they | sie | **sie** | ihnen |
| you, formal | Sie | **Sie** | Ihnen |

## Important reminder

The accusative is usually the direct object, but the most useful approach is still to learn each verb with a sentence chunk:

- **sehen + Akkusativ** -> Ich sehe **ihn**.
- **helfen + Dativ** -> Ich helfe **ihm**.
- **geben + Dativ + Akkusativ** -> Ich gebe **ihm** **das Buch**.

The goal is automatic use, not calculating every sentence.
